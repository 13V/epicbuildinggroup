# What the best Australian builders' sites do, and where they all fall short

Written 11 Nov 2026 from six research briefs (Adelaide; Melbourne; Sydney; Brisbane and Perth;
renovation and heritage specialists; commercial builders and boutique developers). Each agent opened
the sites in a real browser at 1440 and 390 px wide and looked at the screenshots. About 50 sites
were opened; the reports and the kept screenshots (three best and one worst per brief) are in
`competitors/<brief>/`. Nothing here is copied from any site: these are patterns, not designs.

Sites that set the bar: Craig Linke, Urban Habitats and Artesa (Adelaide); Fortem Projects, Builders
of Architecture and Destination Living (Melbourne); MILEHAM, Chateau and TASS (Sydney); Build
Prestige and McCarthy (Brisbane); Azure (Perth); Helix Group, SQ Projects and Hamilton (commercial
and reuse).

## Ten patterns the best share

1. **A real finished project fills the first screen**, as one large photograph or a quiet video,
   never stock. (TASS, Fortem, Build Prestige, MILEHAM.)
2. **One plain sentence says what they build and for whom.** Craig Linke: "boutique, Adelaide based
   building and interior design company. We specialise in architectural builds and custom renovation
   projects." Helix: "One Builder. Every Kind of Space." The weak sites lead with the company name
   or nothing.
3. **A restrained identity**: a warm neutral ground, one serif or fine sans, generous space, large
   images. Nobody good uses bright brand colour as a ground.
4. **Project pages carry facts as data**: suburb, type, year, size, who designed it, scope, status.
   MILEHAM's fact sheet (site, heritage listing, approval pathway, status) and Helix's fact bar
   (type, location, size, value, year, duration) are the high marks.
5. **Projects are told as a story of what was kept and what was solved**: Fortem on restored
   ceiling roses and lacework, Destination Living on a heritage overlay, Build Prestige on a
   stormwater problem fixed mid-build.
6. **Awards are dated, categorised and attached to the project that won them**, not shown as a row
   of logos. (Fortem, Craig Linke, Azure, SQ.)
7. **The licence number sits in the footer of every page**, with memberships near it.
8. **Named people and places**: testimonials attributed by name and suburb, sometimes the client's
   architect; a named director on the contact page ("Call Director Chris Curtain").
9. **The enquiry form asks one or two useful questions**: what kind of project, how far along you
   are (Fortem's stage question is the best), suburb. The best forms use chips or tick boxes, and
   one says when you will hear back (Helix: within one business day).
10. **Motion is restrained and never hijacks the scroll.** Fades, a carousel, a looping video. The
    Adelaide leaders use almost none.

## Five things none of them do well (our opening)

1. **Almost nobody makes it easy to call from a phone.** Across all six briefs only two sites
   (McCarthy, Period Extensions) had a call button on the first phone screen or a fixed call bar. The rest hide the number in the footer or on the
   contact page; several have no `tel:` link at all. Many phone first screens have no action of any
   kind. A builder's client is on a phone, often on site or in the car.
2. **Nobody shows how a renovation actually happened.** One site (Period Extensions) has before
   and after pairs, at the foot of a project page. No plans or drawings, no build stages, almost
   never a duration (Kitchen & Bath Co. gives "8 weeks"), even on sites that specialise in period
   homes. Epic already publishes site photos of footings, frames and pools going in. That is the
   story clients want and nobody tells it.
3. **Process is a list, never a picture.** At best six numbered paragraphs. Nobody shows what the
   client does at each stage, where approvals sit, or that someone meets them on site every week.
   Epic's whole pitch is "regular on site meetings" and a "hands on approach".
4. **Motion decorates; it never explains.** Video loops and fades set a mood. No site uses motion
   to show building: a drawing becoming a house, a plan filling in, a stage arriving. Several pay
   for their effects with blank or grey blocks on first load and very heavy pages.
5. **Nobody presents a builder who does everything, credibly.** The leaders are residential only,
   usually above $1.5m, and their forms say so with a budget floor. Kitchens and bathrooms, small
   developments, commercial fit-outs and heritage reuse on one site, each with real proof, is rare
   (Helix comes closest and is commercial-led). Epic has all of it in its own back catalogue: a
   railway tower into apartments, a cold store into five restaurants, a cafe under the Victoria Park
   grandstand, villas, bungalows and new homes.

## What the worst have in common (to avoid)

The company name as the headline; dark overlays that dull the photographs; SEO page sprawl; project
labels that read like file names ("NORWOOD 3"); template leftovers (another firm's email, placeholder
quotes, a broken captcha); pop-ups over the content; low-contrast pale text; forms that either ask
nothing or ask for a full address and budget before hello.

## The sections our site needs

Home
1. Hero: one sentence on who they are, a finished Epic house that the page builds from a drawing,
   two actions (start a conversation, call Peter). Phone: both actions on the first screen.
2. "What are you planning?": extension, renovation, new home, development, commercial. Choosing one
   changes the examples shown and the first question of the form.
3. Proof strip in their own published facts: licence, 2011 Master Builders SA award, second
   generation, team of 14, 25 years.
4. Feature projects (their four), each with facts, not just a picture.
5. Before and after: a still pair from a real job, the old muted, the new lit.
6. How a build runs: consultation, design, approvals, build, handover, with the site meetings
   drawn as part of it.
7. The Payneham testimonial, as written, given room.
8. Services, briefly, linking to detail.
9. Closing enquiry: the short form and the phone number.

Other pages: projects index with filters and facts on every card; one page per real project told in
order (the brief, what was there, the build in stages, the result, facts, gallery that opens full
screen); services with real examples under each; about and team from their own words; process in
full; contact with the form, the phone, the address and the licence.

On every page: a call button in the header and a fixed call and enquire bar on phones.
